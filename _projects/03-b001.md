B001 is a linear bicycle model with X–Y kinematics and a steering controller: kinematic feedforward plus feedback on yaw rate, heading and lateral error. It runs at constant speed, so it gives no lap time. It is the driver model C001 builds on.

## Model

States $$x = [\beta,\ r,\ X,\ Y,\ \psi]$$, stability derivatives from Race Car Vehicle Dynamics (p.151), linear cornering stiffnesses C<sub>f</sub>, C<sub>r</sub>:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\dot\beta\\ \dot r\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{Y_\beta}{mV} & \frac{Y_r}{mV}-1\\ \frac{N_\beta}{I_z} & \frac{N_r}{I_z}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\beta\\ r\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}\frac{Y_\delta}{mV}\\ \frac{N_\delta}{I_z}\end{bmatrix}\delta
$$

Position and heading, with V<sub>y</sub> = V tan β:

$$
\dot X = V\cos\psi - V_y\sin\psi, \qquad \dot Y = V\sin\psi + V_y\cos\psi, \qquad \dot\psi = r
$$

Steering, kinematic feedforward plus feedback:

$$
\delta = \tan^{-1}(L\kappa) + K_r(V\kappa - r) - K_\psi e_\psi - K_y e_y
$$

Errors are measured at the nearest point on the reference line (X<sub>ref</sub>, Y<sub>ref</sub>, ψ<sub>ref</sub>). Heading error uses the direction of travel ψ+β:

$$
e_\psi = \psi + \beta - \psi_{ref}, \qquad e_y = (Y - Y_{ref})\cos\psi_{ref} - (X - X_{ref})\sin\psi_{ref}
$$

Gains: K<sub>r</sub> 0.32, K<sub>ψ</sub> 7.8, K<sub>y</sub> 2, hand-tuned at the test speed (20 m/s). Integrated with `ode45`.

Car (FSAE parameter sheets): 200 kg, a / b 0.72 / 0.88 m (wheelbase 1.6 m), I<sub>z</sub> 90 kg m², C<sub>f</sub> / C<sub>r</sub> 32,000 / 30,000 N/rad, μ<sub>y</sub> 1.5.

## Key results

**1. The model matches hand calculations.** B001's state-space model against the textbook formulas for the linear bicycle:

| Check | B001 | Hand calc |
|---|---:|---:|
| Understeer gradient | 0.246 °/g | 0.246 °/g |
| Yaw gain r/δ at 10 / 20 / 30 m/s [1/s] | 6.084 / 11.268 / 15.047 | 6.084 / 11.268 / 15.047 |
| Sideslip gain β/δ at 10 / 20 / 30 m/s | 0.353 / −0.180 / −0.913 | 0.353 / −0.180 / −0.913 |
| ω<sub>n</sub>, ζ at 20 m/s | 19.46 rad/s, 0.967 | 19.46 rad/s, 0.967 |

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hand-calc equations</summary>

With L = a + b and understeer gradient K in rad per m/s²:

$$
K = \frac{m}{L}\left(\frac{b}{C_f} - \frac{a}{C_r}\right), \qquad \frac{r}{\delta} = \frac{V/L}{1 + KV^2/L}, \qquad \frac{\beta}{\delta} = \frac{b/L - m a V^2/(L^2 C_r)}{1 + KV^2/L}
$$

$$
\omega_n^2 = \frac{C_f C_r L^2}{m I_z V^2} + \frac{b C_r - a C_f}{I_z}, \qquad 2\zeta\omega_n = \frac{C_f + C_r}{mV} + \frac{a^2 C_f + b^2 C_r}{I_z V}
$$

</details>

**2. Found and fixed: the heading term fought sideslip.** Heading error was taken from the body heading ψ, so the car settled 25.0 mm off a 30 m circle and up to 0.32 m off the test ellipse. Taking it from the direction of travel ψ+β cuts these to 1.4 mm and 15.6 mm. A hand calculation predicts both circle values.

**Hand check.** On a steady circle the velocity is tangent to the path, so the body sits β off it: with heading error from ψ the controller reads e<sub>ψ</sub> = −β, and from ψ+β it reads 0. With r = Vκ, the feedback has to supply only the understeer steer δ<sub>us</sub> = K<sub>us</sub>a<sub>y</sub>:

$$
e_y = -\frac{\delta_{us} + K_\psi e_\psi}{K_y}
$$

30 m circle, 15 m/s (0.76 g), β 0.391°, δ<sub>us</sub> 0.19°:

| Heading error from | Hand calc | Simulation |
|---|---:|---:|
| ψ (before) | +25.0 mm | +25.0 mm |
| ψ+β (after fix) | −1.6 mm | −1.4 mm |

What is left with ψ+β is the understeer steer, which the kinematic feedforward leaves out.

## Limits

Linear tyres never saturate, so there is no grip limit. Constant speed, so no lap time. Gains are fixed and were tuned before the fix. Feeding β through K<sub>ψ</sub> lowers closed-loop damping at speed (lowest ζ from the eigenvalues of the controller and bicycle model linearised on a straight line, states β, r, e<sub>ψ</sub>, e<sub>y</sub>): ζ 0.54 at 20 m/s, 0.22 at 30 m/s and 0.05 at 40 m/s (0.80, 0.49 and 0.34 before). Still stable, but the gains need retuning. The track spline treats the closed track as open, so curvature is wrong within about 1 m of start/finish.
