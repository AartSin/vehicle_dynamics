A001 has no yaw and no driver. B001 adds both: yaw dynamics from the bicycle model, and a steering controller that follows a reference line. It runs at constant speed, so it gives no lap time. It is the step that proves the driver model before C001 adds four wheels and a speed profile.

## Key results

1. **The model matches closed-form results exactly:** understeer gradient 0.246 °/g, yaw and sideslip gains at 10, 20 and 30 m/s, and ω<sub>n</sub> 19.46 rad/s with ζ 0.967 at 20 m/s.
2. **The heading term was fighting sideslip.** On the 30 m circle, the heading error settles at −0.389° while the sideslip is 0.391°. The controller holds the body on the tangent instead of the direction of travel, so the car sits 25 mm off the line. Using ψ+β cuts it to 1.4 mm; tested on a copy, not yet applied.
3. **Track converter from OptimumLap to X–Y.** On FSEC 2025, lap length and net heading match to 0.0003% and curvature correlates at r = 0.9998. OptimumLap's arcs leave a 2.87 m gap at the finish; a smooth correction closes it.
4. **Known limits:** linear tyres never saturate, so B001 cannot see a grip limit (the 20 m/s test ellipse needs 6.2 g). Fixed gains lose damping above 15 m/s (ζ 0.34 at 40 m/s).
